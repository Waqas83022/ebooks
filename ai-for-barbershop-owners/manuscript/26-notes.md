## Introduction

The barbershop blood pressure study: Ronald G. Victor and colleagues, "A Cluster-Randomized Trial of Blood-Pressure Reduction in Black Barbershops," *New England Journal of Medicine* 378 (2018): 1291–1301. The trial enrolled 319 Black men with high blood pressure from 52 barbershops in Los Angeles County. At six months, mean systolic pressure fell by 27.0 mm Hg in the intervention group and 9.3 mm Hg in the control group. Free full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6018053/

The Air Canada chatbot case: *Moffatt v. Air Canada*, 2024 BCCRT 149, decided by the Civil Resolution Tribunal of British Columbia in February 2024. The tribunal found the airline responsible for incorrect bereavement-fare information given by the chatbot on its website and awarded the passenger $650.88 in damages, plus interest and fees, about C$812 in total. Decisions are published at https://decisions.civilresolutionbc.ca

New and existing guest visits at barbershops: Zenoti, "Barbershop Trends 2026: 6 Revenue Growth Strategies," published 19 May 2026, drawing on Zenoti's 2026 Beauty and Wellness Benchmark Report. The page reports a 17% same-store decline in new-guest visits at barbershops in 2025 against an industry average of 10%, and 2% growth in existing-guest visits. https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/barbershop-trends-2026. These are figures from businesses that use Zenoti's software in North America; the company notes that year-over-year comparisons are directional because the group of businesses changes between editions.

## Chapter 6

Federal Trade Commission, "Federal Trade Commission Announces Final Rule Banning Fake Reviews and Testimonials," press release, 14 August 2024, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials. The release lists the practices covered: fake or false reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones; buying positive or negative reviews through incentives conditioned on sentiment; undisclosed insider reviews; company-controlled review sites; review suppression; and fake social media indicators.

Google's policy on incentives for reviews: Google Business Profile Help, "Tips to get more reviews," https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122. The page says that offering incentives such as free or discounted goods or services in exchange for posting, changing or removing reviews is considered fake and misleading content and is strictly prohibited, and links to Google's Maps user-contributed content policy.

## Chapter 10

Rebooking and cancellation figures, no-show and cancellation rates for barbershops (4% each in 2025), and membership growth are from the Zenoti page cited for the introduction. The rebooking table on that page covers "salon businesses, which includes barbershops".

Quiet hours for telephone solicitations (not before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. at the called party's location): 47 CFR § 64.1200(c)(1), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-64/subpart-L/section-64.1200. The same section sets out the consent requirements for telemarketing calls and texts. The rules have been interpreted by courts and amended by the FCC many times; this book describes them only in general terms.

Published no-show rates for barbershops vary widely depending on who collects them and how they are counted. Booking-software vendors publish many of the figures, often without full methods. That is why Chapter 2 asks you to measure your own.

## Chapter 11

Gift card expiry: in the United States, the Credit CARD Act of 2009 and the Federal Reserve's Regulation E (12 CFR § 1005.20) generally prohibit gift cards from expiring within five years of issue or the last time money was loaded. Some states go further.

Membership sales growth at barbershops (20% in 2025): Zenoti page cited for the introduction.

## Chapter 12

Worker classification rules differ by country and, in the United States, by state. The US Department of Labor and state labor departments publish guidance on independent contractors; an accountant or employment lawyer can tell you how the rules apply to your arrangements.
