# Epilogue: Why try? {.unnumbered}

We began with a question, and I promised you a careful answer. Here it is, as plainly as I can give it.

*If hard work cannot guarantee the result, if luck can undo the best preparation, if destiny may already be written and if prayers are sometimes answered with a no, then why try at all?*

Try, because effort is the one vote you always get to cast, even in contests you cannot control.

Try, because opportunity has to find you somewhere, and it almost never finds people who are standing still.

Try, because if anything is written, your trying is written too. And if nothing is written, your trying is how anything gets written at all.

Try, because a missed arrow ends in grief, and grief, given time and company, eases. An arrow never released ends in a question, and questions do not ease. They wait.

Try, because of what the trying makes of you: the skill, the nerve, the friends met on the way, the larger room you live in afterward, the self you discover only when you want something badly enough to go after it.

Try, because hope without action is only a wish, and action without hope is only a habit. Together they are the closest thing a human being has to a hand on the future.

And try, finally, because the outcome was never yours to own. The aim always was.

* * *

At the start of this book, I mentioned a song whose chorus admits that all the trying, all the distance covered, came to nothing in the end. I understand why people sing it. I have felt it. On the night the email arrives, or the conversation in the café ends, or the letter from the embassy is opened, it can feel like the truest thing anyone has ever said.

I do not believe it anymore.

It mattered. Not because it guaranteed anything, but because it was real. You prepared, and the preparation became part of you. You loved, and the love was not wasted because it was not returned. You hoped, and the hope carried you through days that would otherwise have stopped you. Perhaps you prayed, and the prayer steadied your hands. None of it bought the result. All of it was yours.

In the end, it does matter. Not because of where the arrow landed, but because of who drew the bow.

* * *

I want to leave you with a picture.

It is early morning in a wide field. The grass is wet. Far down the field there is a target, small at this distance, and you are not sure you can reach it. You have missed it before. Some of your arrows are still lying in the grass where they fell.

You walk to the line. You set your feet. You feel the wind on your face, and you know you cannot stop it; you can only learn to read it. You do not know what is written about this arrow, and you will not know until it lands. You take a breath, and whether you send it upward or simply let it go into the cold air, it steadies you. That breath is hope. It is not certainty. It was never meant to be.

Then you draw. Not because the arrow is guaranteed to hit. Because you are an archer, and this is what archers do.

The wind is blowing. The page is open. The target is far away.

Breathe. Draw.
