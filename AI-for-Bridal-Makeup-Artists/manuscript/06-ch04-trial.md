# 4. Turn the bridal trial into a wedding-day brief {#ch4}

Amara's trial goes beautifully. She arrives at the studio on a Saturday morning in August with a photo of her dress on her phone and a folder of screenshots she's been saving since spring. You talk for twenty minutes, try a warm bronze eye, soften the liner, swap the lip twice and step out into the parking lot so she can see her skin in daylight. At the end she looks in the mirror for a long moment and says, "That's it. Exactly that."

You take a few photos, scribble on a face chart, say goodbye and start setting up for your afternoon client.

Seven weeks later, at 9:50 on her wedding morning, you open your kit and try to remember what "exactly that" was. You remember the bronze. You think you remember softening the liner, but was it at the outer corner or all the way along? Was the second lip color the one she chose, or the one she nearly chose? And didn't she say something about her maid of honor wanting lashes?

The trial produced decisions. The problem is that most of them were made out loud, in a friendly conversation, and nobody wrote them down in a form that would still make sense in October.

This chapter turns the trial into two documents. The first is a short recap you send to the bride within a day, so she can confirm what was agreed and what's still open. The second is a wedding-day look brief for you (and for any artist working with you), with everything you'll need on the morning in one place.

## What the trial is for

A trial is a makeup appointment, but from a booking point of view it's a decision meeting. By the end of it, you want answers to four questions:

1. What's the agreed look, in words you'll understand later?
2. What are you changing from what you did at the trial?
3. What does the bride still need to decide or confirm?
4. Did anything at the trial change the timing or the price?

The first three are about the makeup. The fourth is about the booking, and it's the one most artists forget. If the trial look took 80 minutes to apply and your wedding-day plan allows 60, you have a problem that's better solved in August than on the morning. If the bride decided at the trial that her mother wants makeup after all, or that all four bridesmaids would like lashes, that's a change of scope with a price attached. It should go through the same steps as any other change (Chapter 6), not simply appear on the day.

## Before the trial: one useful message

Send a short message a few days before the trial. Its job is to make sure she arrives with what you need and without anything that slows you down.

::: prompt
**Copy-ready prompt**

Use my studio brief. Lead L-031 has a bridal trial at my studio on [date] at [time], 90 minutes, already paid. Draft a short reminder message (under 110 words). Ask her to bring: a photo of her dress, any inspiration photos, and her hair plans if she has them. Suggest she wears a top with a similar neckline to her dress. Ask her to tell me about any allergies or skin sensitivities when she arrives, or on my consultation form, rather than by message. Give the studio address as [Studio address]. No extra instructions beyond these.
:::

Notice the line about allergies and skin. You need that information, and it's important for her safety. But it's health information, and it belongs in your consultation form or your client record, not in a DM thread and never in a chat tool. Asking her to bring it to the appointment, or to put it on the form you already use, keeps it in the right place from the start.

> Hi Amara, looking forward to your trial on Saturday at 10:00. A few things that will help: a photo of your dress, any inspiration photos you love, and your hair plans if you have them. If you can, wear a top with a similar neckline to your dress so we can see the whole look together. Please tell me about any allergies or skin sensitivities when you arrive, or add them to the consultation form I sent. The studio is at [Studio address]. See you soon.

## During the trial: capture it in your words

You're busy during a trial. Your hands are full and you're watching her face, not a notebook. So capture as you go in whatever way is easiest, and tidy it up straight after.

Some artists use a paper face chart. Some talk into a voice memo while the bride is looking at herself in daylight. Some type three words into their phone between steps. Any of these works, as long as it happens before the next client arrives. Memory fades quickly in a busy week, and a trial you remember clearly on Saturday is a blur by Wednesday.

Write down the things only you will understand. "Liner shorter, stop at end of lash line, smudge outer third." "Shimmer only center lid, she's worried about flash." "Blush up one step, outdoors at 3." These are your notes, and they can be as cryptic as you like.

Keep identifiable photos in the place your studio uses for client records, whether that's a folder in your booking system, a dedicated album, or whatever you've chosen and told your clients about. Not your camera roll next to your vacation photos, and not in a chat tool.

## Turning notes into a recap the bride can confirm

Within a day of the trial, send the bride a recap. It does three jobs. It shows her you listened. It gives her a chance to correct anything you misheard before it becomes a wedding-day surprise. And it gives you a written record of what was agreed.

A good recap has three sections, and they're kept separate for a reason:

- **What we agreed.** The look in plain words, with any products or shades she chose.
- **What I'll adjust on the day.** The changes from the trial.
- **What you still need to confirm.** Anything undecided, with a date if it matters.

![The trial recap has three parts. Keep them separate so the bride can see what is settled and what is still open.](images/fig-04-trial-recap.png){.figure}

The AI is good at this job, because it's a language task: taking your shorthand and turning it into something clear and warm. It's also good at noticing when something in your notes is undecided, which you might skim over.

Before you hand your notes to the AI, remove anything sensitive. That means her name, any health or skin information, and anything about her body you'd only discuss in person. You don't need those in the recap anyway. If a note matters for safety, such as an allergy, it lives in her client record, and your own wedding-day brief can simply say "See client record."

Here are your trial notes for Amara, after you've taken out the sensitive parts:

> L-031 trial. Soft glam, skin-focused. Warm bronze on lid, deeper in crease. Liner: started full length, she preferred shorter, stop at end of lashes, smudged outer third. Shimmer center lid only (flash). Half strip lashes, she liked them. Brows: brushed up, fill tails only. Blush: rosy, went up a step after daylight check, outdoors photos 3pm. Lip: tried Rose Clay and Petal, leaning Rose Clay but wants to see with dress fabric in daylight. Foundation: mix of two shades (on face chart). Setting: light powder T-zone only. Asked if MOH can have lashes too, maybe other bridesmaids, she'll ask them. Timing: application 55 min, plus 10 min swapping lip and adjusting liner.

::: prompt
**Copy-ready prompt**

Turn my trial notes below into a short recap email for the bride. Use three headings: "What we agreed," "What I'll adjust on the day," and "What I'd like you to confirm." Plain language she'll understand, no makeup jargon she wouldn't know. Keep every product name and decision exactly as written. Don't add products, techniques or claims about longevity. If a note is undecided, put it under "What I'd like you to confirm." If adding services would change the price, say so and use the rates in my brief. Under 200 words. Use [Client first name]. Then, separately, list anything in my notes that looks like it affects timing.

[Paste your anonymised trial notes]
:::

The draft comes back. Read it against your notes, not just against the checklist. AI tools like to tidy things up, and sometimes the tidying changes the meaning. "Shimmer center lid only" can turn into "a soft shimmer across the lid," which is the opposite of what she asked for. After checking, you send:

> Hi Amara,
>
> Thank you for Saturday. It was lovely to see the look come together. I've put my notes below so we're both working from the same plan.
>
> **What we agreed**
> A soft, skin-focused look with a warm bronze eye, a little deeper in the crease. Half lashes. Brows brushed up with the tails filled in. A rosy blush and a light powder only where you need it.
>
> **What I'll adjust on the day**
> Liner shorter than we started with, stopping at the end of your lashes and softened at the outer corner. Shimmer only in the center of the lid, so it stays soft in flash photos. Blush one step deeper than the first version, for your outdoor photos.
>
> **What I'd like you to confirm**
> Your lip color: Rose Clay or Petal, once you've seen them with your dress fabric in daylight. And whether your maid of honor, or any of your other bridesmaids, would like lashes. They're $15 each and add about five minutes per person, so I'd love to know by September 26 to plan the timing.
>
> [Artist first name]

Two details there are worth pointing out.

The lash question has a price and a deadline. The AI correctly pulled the $15 rate from your brief, and you added the date yourself. A date makes the question answerable. Without one, "let me know about lashes" tends to be answered at 8:15 on the wedding morning, by a bridesmaid sitting in your chair.

The recap doesn't say anything about her skin, her allergies or the foundation shades. The shade mix is on your face chart, in your client record, and nothing about it needs her approval. A recap only includes what the bride needs to read or decide.

### What the AI noticed about timing

Underneath the draft, the AI listed two timing points from your notes. First, the application took 55 minutes, plus 10 minutes of adjustments. Your plan allows 60 minutes for the bride. On the day there won't be any swapping, so 55 minutes is realistic, but there's very little spare. Second, if all four bridesmaids add lashes, that's 20 more minutes on a morning that already has no slack.

Both points are right, and both go straight into your tracker as notes for Chapter 5. If you'd only had the AI write the recap and never asked about timing, you might not have connected "she's asking about lashes for her bridesmaids" with "my schedule has zero minutes spare."

## When the bride replies

Amara replies two days later. She's chosen Rose Clay. Her maid of honor would like lashes; the others would rather not. That's one $15 add-on and five extra minutes.

Treat this as a small change to a booked job. It doesn't need the full change process from Chapter 6, but it does need three things done properly:

- **Update the scope.** Bride plus four bridesmaids, plus lashes for one. Note it in the tracker.
- **Update the money.** Your brief says changes agreed before the balance is due are added to the balance. The balance goes from $480 to $495. Update the tracker so the figure you ask for in late September is the right one.
- **Update the timing.** Attendant 1 (if the maid of honor goes first) becomes a 45-minute service. That's five minutes your schedule doesn't have. You'll fix it when you build the full timeline in Chapter 5, but note it now.

Then reply to confirm, so it's in writing:

> Hi Amara, perfect, Rose Clay it is. I've added lashes for your maid of honor, which is $15, so your balance is now $495, still due a week before the wedding. I'll include her extra five minutes in the running order I send you.

## The wedding-day look brief

The recap is for the bride. The look brief is for you. It's an internal document, a single page you'll open on the wedding morning (and send to an associate artist, if one is working with you). It pulls together everything from the booking, the trial and any changes since, so nobody has to scroll back through months of messages at 6:30 a.m.

::: template
**WEDDING-DAY LOOK BRIEF: L-031**

*Version 2. Updated after trial recap reply.*

**Date and timing.** October 10. Room access 7:00. Ready-by 11:15. Running order: see timeline v[number].

**Services.** Bride. Four bridesmaids. Lashes for maid of honor (+$15, added to balance). Balance $495, due October 3.

**Bride's look.** Soft glam, skin-focused. Warm bronze lid, deeper crease. Shimmer center lid only. Liner stops at end of lashes, outer third smudged. Half strip lashes. Brows brushed up, fill tails only. Blush rosy, one step deeper than first trial version. Lip: Rose Clay. Light powder T-zone only.

**Base.** Shade mix and skin notes: see client record and face chart.

**Bridesmaids.** Bride's guidance: soft and coordinated with her look, not identical. Neutral lip, no heavy liner. Individual preferences collected via the bride by [date].

**Touch-up kit for bride.** Rose Clay lipstick, blotting papers, small powder.

**Kit additions for this job.** Half lashes (bride), strip lashes (MOH), Rose Clay, Petal as backup.

**Open questions.** None. (Updated [date].)

**Photos.** Trial photos in client file L-031.
:::

There's nothing sensitive in the brief that wouldn't already be in your client record. If you're sending it to an associate artist, send it through the same channel you use for other work documents, and let the bride know (in your agreement or privacy notice) that associates may see booking details. You'll find a blank version in Appendix A.

You can ask the AI to build the look brief from your trial notes, recap and the bride's reply. It's good at merging three sources into one page, and at spotting a contradiction between them, such as "lip: Rose Clay" in one place and "lip: undecided" in another. Read it once more against the bride's actual reply before you save it. If the brief and the client disagree, the client's written confirmation wins.

## Bridesmaids, mothers and everyone else

The trial is usually just the bride, but the wedding-day chair holds four or five other people with their own preferences. Gathering those in advance saves time on the morning and avoids the bridesmaid who sits down and says "I never wear eyeliner" after you've finished her lids.

The simplest way is a short message the bride forwards to her party. Keep it to the facts you need to plan the morning and the work, and ask them to reply to the bride or planner, or to you directly by email, rather than in a big group chat.

> Hi everyone, I'm [Artist first name], doing makeup for Amara's wedding on October 10. To make the morning smooth, could you let me know by September 26:
>
> 1. Whether you'd like lashes ($15 each)
> 2. Anything you'd definitely like or definitely not like (for example, no liner, or a lip color you love)
> 3. Any allergies or skin sensitivities I should know about. You can tell me in person on the morning if you'd rather.
>
> See you on the day!

This is a short message to draft yourself. If you'd like the AI to help with tone, give it the three questions and the deadline and ask for a friendly version under 90 words.

## Ten-minute action

Find your notes from the most recent trial you've done. If there are no notes, write down what you remember now, and notice how much is already fuzzy.

Remove anything sensitive and run them through the recap prompt in this chapter. Compare the three sections to what you actually agreed. If the wedding hasn't happened yet, send the recap to the bride with a short note: "I've put our trial notes in one place so we're both working from the same plan." Then create a look brief for that booking, even a rough one, and save it with the booking.
