# Appendix B: Three practice bookings {#appendix-b}

Reading about a process is one thing. Running it on a booking where you don't already know the answer is another. Here are three fictional bookings for the same studio as the rest of the book, using the studio brief from Chapter 1.

For each one, work through it before you read the answer. Do the timing first, then the price, then decide what you'd say. If you want to test your AI setup at the same time, give it the facts and the prompts from Appendix A and compare its answer with yours and with the worked answer.

The studio's key numbers, so you don't have to turn back:

- Bride $220 (60 min). Attendant or family $95 (40 min). Junior $50 (20 min). Attendant lashes $15 (adds 5 min). Trial $110.
- Setup 15 min. Buffer 20 min for up to 5 services, 30 min for 6 or more. Maximum 6 services per artist. Earliest start 6:00 a.m.
- Travel per artist: Zone 1 (to 25 miles) $40. Zone 2 (25 to 50 miles) $75.
- Wedding-day minimum $300 before travel. Deposit 25% of the wedding-day total, rounded up to the nearest $10.

## Practice booking 1: The five-person bridal party

**The inquiry.** Lead L-061 arrives through your website form, complete. The wedding is on Saturday, May 16. The bride is getting ready at a farmhouse 35 miles from your studio. The room is available from 8:30 a.m. Everyone must be ready by 12:30 p.m. for photos. Makeup is for the bride, three bridesmaids and the mother of the bride. One bridesmaid would like lashes. The bride is interested in a trial. May 16 is open in your calendar.

**Your task.** Check the timing. Calculate the quote, deposit and balance. Decide what to say about anything that doesn't fit.

### Worked answer

**Timing.** Five services, so a 20-minute buffer.

- Setup: 15
- Bride: 60
- Four attendants and family at 40: 160
- Lashes for one bridesmaid: 5
- Buffer: 20
- **Total: 260 minutes**

The window from 8:30 to 12:30 is 240 minutes. You're **20 minutes short.** Working backward from 12:30, your latest arrival is 8:10.

This is the most common kind of problem, and it's the reason the timing check comes before the price. It's also very easy to fix at this stage. Before you quote, ask: "Could I get into the room at 8:00? I need a little more time than 8:30 allows for five people." If the farmhouse agrees, the morning fits with 10 minutes to spare. If not, you'll offer the options: earlier access elsewhere in the house, a second artist for part of the morning or a later ready-by time.

**Price.**

- Bride: $220
- Three bridesmaids and the mother of the bride at $95: $380
- Lashes for one bridesmaid: $15
- Travel, Zone 2: $75
- **Wedding-day total: $690**

Minimum: services are $615, above the $300 minimum.

Deposit: 25% of $690 is $172.50. Rounded up to the nearest $10, that's **$180**. Balance: **$510**. The trial is quoted separately at $110.

**What you'd send.** Because the timing problem affects whether you can do the booking at all, you can either ask about access first and quote afterwards, or quote with the access condition written in. With a complete inquiry from a website form, quoting straight away keeps the momentum. Something like:

> I've penciled in an 8:00 a.m. setup and a 12:30 p.m. finish. For five people I need to start a little earlier than 8:30, so could you check with the farmhouse whether I can get into the room at 8:00? If that's not possible, I'll suggest a couple of alternatives.

**What the AI often gets wrong here.** It may use Zone 1 travel ($40) because that's the one it's seen most often in your examples. It may calculate the deposit as $172.50 or round it to $170, rather than up to $180. And it may lay out a schedule that "fits" by starting at 8:30 and dropping the buffer to zero. If yours did any of these, add a line to your brief: "Always state which travel zone you used. Always show the deposit calculation."

## Practice booking 2: The bride-only elopement

**The inquiry.** Lead L-062, by email. The bride is eloping on a Thursday, July 9, at the city hall, with two witnesses. She's getting ready at a hotel 12 miles away. The hotel room is hers from 11:00 a.m. and she needs to be ready by 1:00 p.m. Makeup is for her only. No trial; she says she knows what she likes. July 9 is open.

**Your task.** Check the timing. Calculate the quote. Think about how to explain anything that might surprise her.

### Worked answer

**Timing.** One service, so a 20-minute buffer. Setup 15, bride 60, buffer 20: 95 minutes. The window is 120 minutes. You have 25 minutes to spare. You could arrive at 11:15 and still finish comfortably, or arrive at 11:00 and give her a slower, calmer start.

**Price.** Bridal makeup is $220. But the wedding-day minimum is $300 before travel, so the services are charged at **$300**. Add Zone 1 travel of $40. **Wedding-day total: $340.**

Deposit: 25% of $340 is $85. Rounded up to the nearest $10: **$90**. Balance: **$250**.

**Explaining the minimum.** This is the part that needs care. A bride who has seen "$220 for bridal" on your price list will be surprised by a quote that says $300. Explain it once, plainly, and don't apologize for it:

> Bridal makeup is $220. For wedding-day bookings I have a minimum of $300, which covers the time I set aside for the day, so the makeup total for your elopement is $300, plus $40 for travel.

**A decision you might offer.** With $80 of the minimum unused, some artists would offer something in that space. Makeup for one of her witnesses is $95, which would bring the services to $315 and use the time well. Whether to suggest it is a decision, not a rule, so it's not in the AI's job. If you decide to offer it, write it in yourself:

> If either of your witnesses would like makeup too, that's $95, and your total would be $355.

That's $315 of services plus $40 travel. The deposit on $355 would be $88.75, rounded up to $90, the same as before.

**What the AI often gets wrong here.** The most common mistake is to ignore the minimum and quote $260 ($220 plus $40). The second is to apply the minimum to the total including travel and quote $300 flat. Your brief says "$300 before travel," so the correct figure is $340. If the AI gets this wrong, make the minimum a line it has to check: "Always check the wedding-day minimum and state whether it applies."

## Practice booking 3: The two-artist wedding with a late venue opening

**The booking.** Lead L-063 booked in January for Saturday, September 19. The bride and seven bridesmaids want makeup. The getting-ready venue is a hotel 20 miles away. Room access was 8:00 a.m. and everyone must be ready by 12:00. Because there are eight services, you quoted two artists: you and your associate. The booking is confirmed.

Three weeks before the wedding, the planner emails: the hotel has a breakfast event in the suite and can't release it until 8:30.

**Your task.** Work out what the booking cost and how the two artists' work was split. Then work out what the late opening does, and what you'd offer.

### Worked answer

**The original price.**

- Bride: $220
- Seven bridesmaids at $95: $665
- Travel, Zone 1, two artists at $40: $80
- **Wedding-day total: $965**

Deposit: 25% of $965 is $241.25, rounded up to **$250**. Balance: **$715**.

**The original split.** Eight services means a 30-minute buffer for each artist. The bride stays with you.

- You: bride plus three bridesmaids, 180 minutes of services. Setup 15, services 180, buffer 30: 225 minutes. From 8:00 to 12:00 is 240. Fits, with 15 to spare.
- Associate: four bridesmaids, 160 minutes. Setup 15, services 160, buffer 30: 205 minutes. Latest arrival 8:35. Fits.

**The late opening.** With access at 8:30 the window is 210 minutes.

- You: you need 225. **You're 15 minutes short.**
- Associate: she needs 205. **She still fits, with 5 minutes to spare.**

Notice that the problem is only in your column. That changes the options.

**Option 1: Rebalance.** Move one bridesmaid from you to your associate. You'd have the bride and two bridesmaids: 15 + 140 + 30 = 185 minutes, which fits. But your associate would have five bridesmaids: 15 + 200 + 30 = 245 minutes, which is 35 minutes too long. Rebalancing doesn't work here.

**Option 2: Early access for one artist.** Ask the hotel whether one person can start at 8:15 in a different room, such as a spare bedroom, or the bride's own room. You'd need 15 minutes. That's a small request, and hotels often say yes to one supplier with a small kit. No extra cost.

**Option 3: A third artist for one service.** If no early room is available, a third artist could take one bridesmaid. You'd have the bride and two (185 minutes, fits), your associate keeps four (205 minutes, fits), and the third artist does one service: 15 + 40 + 30 = 85 minutes, arriving by 10:35. The cost to the client is an extra $40 travel fee, making the total $1,005 and the balance $755.

**Option 4: Talk to the photographer.** If the bride could be ready at 12:15 while the bridesmaids stay at 12:00, your column might fit without any other change. Worth asking, though it depends on the photographer and the order of the day.

**What you'd send.** Lead with the free option, and show you've already done the work:

> Hi [Planner], thanks for letting me know about the 8:30 opening. My associate's schedule still fits, but mine is 15 minutes short, so I want to solve it now. The simplest fix would be for me to start at 8:15 in any other room, even a spare bedroom. Could you ask the hotel? If that's not possible, I can bring a third artist for one bridesmaid, which would add $40 for travel. I'll wait to hear from you before changing anything.

Then record it on a change request form (Appendix A), so that whichever option you agree is approved in writing before you send version 2 of the schedule.

**What the AI often gets wrong here.** Asked to "fix the schedule for the 8:30 opening," a chat tool will usually rebalance the services evenly and present a plan that looks tidy. Check it against the sums: an even split puts your associate 35 minutes over. It may also shorten the buffer to 15 minutes "to make it work." Ask instead for the minutes needed and available for each artist, and decide the fix yourself.
