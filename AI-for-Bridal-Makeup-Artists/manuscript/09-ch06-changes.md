# 6. Handle late changes without overbooking {#ch6}

On Thursday, October 1, nine days before the wedding, an email from Dana, the planner, lands at 9:12 p.m.:

"Hi! Two quick things for Amara's wedding. Her mom has decided she'd love makeup too, if that's OK? And Theo has asked if we can move the bridal party portraits to 10:45 instead of 11:15. The light in the walled garden is better earlier and it gives us more breathing room before the ceremony. Hope that works! Dana"

It's a friendly email, and both requests are reasonable. "If that's OK?" and "Hope that works!" invite a quick "Of course!" in reply. It's late, you're tired, and you like this family. Saying yes would take ten seconds.

It would also commit you to a morning that doesn't fit. And you'd only find that out next week, when you sit down to update the schedule, or on the morning itself, at 10:20, with the mother of the bride still waiting for her turn.

This chapter is about the pause between reading a change and answering it. It's a short pause, usually no more than half an hour of work. In that time you work out what the change does to staffing, cost and timing, get the client's agreement to the new scope, and send an updated schedule with a new version date. The AI can help a great deal with the wording. It can't tell you whether to say yes.

## Every change touches three things

Before replying to any change, run it through three questions:

1. **Timing.** Does the morning still fit, with setup and buffer intact?
2. **Staffing.** Can you still do it alone, or does it need another artist, an earlier start or a change of plan?
3. **Cost.** Does the price change? What's the new total and balance?

Most late changes touch more than one. A new person in the chair is a cost change, but it's usually also a timing change, and sometimes a staffing change. A new ready-by time costs nothing on its own, but it can make the morning impossible for one artist, which means a staffing change, which means a cost change.

Work through each change separately first, then together. Two changes that each fit on their own can be impossible together.

## Amara's two changes, one at a time

Start with the current plan, version 3: you alone, room access at 6:45, setup, then the maid of honor (45 minutes with lashes), three bridesmaids at 40 minutes, the bride at 60, and a buffer, all finished by 11:15.

**Change A: Amara's mother wants makeup.** That's one more 40-minute service. The party now has six services, so your brief calls for a 30-minute buffer instead of 20. You need 15 minutes of setup, 265 minutes of services and 30 minutes of buffer: 310 minutes. From 6:45 to 11:15 you have 270. You're 40 minutes short. Six services is also your maximum for one artist, so you're at the limit even before timing.

**Change B: Ready-by moves to 10:45.** No new services, so the buffer stays at 20. You need 15 + 225 + 20 = 260 minutes. From 6:45 to 10:45 you have 240. You're 20 minutes short.

**Both together.** You need 310 minutes and have 240. You're 70 minutes short.

![Each change on its own, and both together. Two small changes can add up to a morning one artist can't do.](images/fig-06-change-impact.png){.figure}

Seventy minutes is not something you fix by working faster. You have four options, and it's worth writing them down before you choose:

- **Start earlier.** You'd need to be in the room at 5:35. That's before your 6:00 earliest start, so it needs your approval and carries a $60 early-start fee. It also needs the venue to agree, and Jess's hair schedule would have the same problem.
- **Add a second artist.** Your associate, Nadia, takes some of the services. Cost: her services are charged at your normal rates, plus a second $40 travel fee.
- **Change the scope.** Perhaps Amara's mother doesn't need to be in the 10:45 portraits and could have her makeup done later. Or perhaps the portraits could start with the bride and maid of honor, and the others join at 11:00.
- **Say yes to one change and not the other.** Keep the 11:15 portraits and add the mother with a second artist, or move to 10:45 and decline the mother's makeup.

Which of these to offer is a decision. It depends on whether Nadia is free (you message her: she is, from 8:00), whether you're willing to start before 6:00 (you'd rather not), and what you know about this family. You decide to offer the second artist as your recommended option, and mention that the alternative is to keep one change and not the other.

## Ask the AI to draft, and only to draft

Now you have the facts and a decision. This is where the AI earns its keep, because the reply has to explain a change in price to a planner who was hoping for "Of course!" without sounding like a refusal.

::: prompt
**Copy-ready prompt**

Use my studio brief. Lead L-031. The planner has asked for two changes nine days before the wedding: (1) add makeup for the mother of the bride; (2) move the ready-by time from 11:15 to 10:45. I've checked: with both changes, one artist is 70 minutes short. My associate is available from 8:00 a.m.

Draft a reply to the planner (under 170 words). Say yes to both changes if the client approves adding a second artist. Give the cost: mother of the bride $95, second artist travel $40, so $135 added. The new total is $790 and the new balance is $630, still due October 3. Say I'll send updated chair times (version 4) once the client approves. Mention the alternative: keep one change only and stay with one artist. Ask her to confirm in writing which option the client wants. Warm and clear, no apology. Don't promise anything else.
:::

Before you look at the draft, check the numbers yourself. The original total was $640. Lashes for the maid of honor added $15, making $655. The mother of the bride adds $95 and the second artist's travel adds $40, so the new total is $790. The deposit was $160, so the balance is $630. If your numbers and the prompt agree, read the draft.

> Hi Dana,
>
> Happy to help with both. Here's what they mean for the morning.
>
> With Amara's mom added and everyone ready by 10:45, the morning is about 70 minutes too long for one artist, so I'd bring in my associate, Nadia, for part of it. That way nobody is rushed and the buffer stays in place.
>
> The changes would add $135: $95 for Amara's mom's makeup and $40 for Nadia's travel. The new total would be $790, and the balance $630, still due on October 3.
>
> If Amara would rather stay with one artist, we could make one of the two changes instead: either add her mom and keep 11:15, or move to 10:45 without adding her mom.
>
> Could you confirm in writing which option Amara would like? As soon as I have that, I'll send the updated chair times as version 4.
>
> [Artist first name]

Read the draft for promises. There's one you should look at closely: "add her mom and keep 11:15." With the mother added and an 11:15 deadline, you'd be 40 minutes short, so even that option needs Nadia. The AI wrote what you told it to write, but your instruction was too loose. You edit the alternative to "move to 10:45 without adding her mom (no change in price), or add her mom with Nadia's help and keep 11:15." The price of the second option is the same $135, so you say that too.

This is why you read the draft against your own arithmetic, not just against the checklist. The tool did its job. You found the gap in your own instructions.

## Get approval before you change anything

Dana replies the next morning: Amara would love both changes, with Nadia. Good news, but it came from the planner, and the person who pays and signs the agreement is Amara.

Whether you need approval from the client herself, or whether the planner can approve changes on her behalf, depends on your agreement and on how the booking was made. Many artists simply copy the client in and ask for a one-line reply. It takes a minute and it avoids the conversation in which a client says she never agreed to an extra $135.

> Hi Amara (copying Dana),
>
> Just confirming the change to your booking: makeup for your mom, everyone ready by 10:45, with my associate Nadia joining for part of the morning. That adds $135, making your total $790 and your balance $630, due October 3. Could you reply "approved" so I can update everything?

Amara replies "Approved!" within the hour. Now, in this order:

1. Record the approval in your tracker, with the date, and update the services, total and balance.
2. Update the agreement if your terms require it, using your normal contract system.
3. Confirm with Nadia: date, arrival time, services, rate and where to park.
4. Rebuild the schedule and send it to Jess for the person view.
5. Send version 4 to Dana and Amara.

The order matters. Don't send a new schedule before the client has approved the new price, or you'll have committed to the staffing without agreement on the cost.

## Rebuilding the schedule

The revised morning, version 4, has two makeup artists. Work backward from 10:45 for each, as in Chapter 5, with the 30-minute buffer that six services call for.

You keep the bride, the maid of honor and Bridesmaid 2. Your services add up to 145 minutes. Nadia takes Bridesmaids 3 and 4 and Amara's mother: 120 minutes. Nadia's latest start is 10:45, minus 30 minutes of buffer, minus 120 minutes of services, minus 15 minutes of setup: 8:00. That's exactly when she's available, so there's no slack in her column.

You still have access from 6:45, which gives you room to leave a gap in the middle of your morning for something you can't move: the bride's hair. With an earlier deadline, Jess needs Amara in her chair earlier, so she's bringing an assistant to take two of the bridesmaids. She asks for Amara from 8:15 to 9:15.

**You (lead artist), version 4**

- 6:45--7:00 Setup
- 7:00--7:45 Maid of honor (with lashes)
- 7:45--8:25 Bridesmaid 2
- 8:25--9:15 Free (bride in hair; kit prep for bride)
- 9:15--10:15 Bride
- 10:15--10:45 Touch-ups and buffer

**Nadia (associate), version 4**

- 8:00--8:15 Setup
- 8:15--8:55 Bridesmaid 3
- 8:55--9:35 Bridesmaid 4
- 9:35--10:15 Mother of the bride
- 10:15--10:45 Touch-ups and buffer

![Version 4 of the morning. Two makeup artists, an earlier deadline and room for the bride's hair in the middle.](images/fig-06-revised-timeline.png){.figure}

Send both columns to Jess and ask for the person view (the prompt is in Chapter 5). She and her assistant rearrange the bridesmaids' hair around your chair times. One clash shows up: Jess's assistant had Bridesmaid 3 in her chair at 8:15, the same time as Nadia. They move Bridesmaid 3's hair later and give the slot to Bridesmaid 2, who is free from 8:25. A two-minute fix in a document is a twenty-minute problem on the morning.

Then make the short bridal party version, with a new heading: "Version 4, updated October 2." Send it to Dana with a note that it replaces version 3. Ask her to delete or clearly mark the old one wherever she's shared it.

## The change request form

You won't always get changes as a tidy email. They come in voice notes, in comments under an Instagram post and from bridesmaids who message you directly. A short form, kept in your notes app, makes sure you capture each one the same way before you reply.

::: template
**CHANGE REQUEST: [Lead ID]**

**Requested by:** [Bride / planner / other] on [date], via [channel]

**The change, in one line:** [e.g., add mother of the bride; ready-by moves to 10:45]

**Timing.** Minutes needed now: [ ] Minutes available now: [ ] Short or spare: [ ]

**Staffing.** One artist still OK? [Yes / No]. If no: [second artist / earlier start / scope change]

**Cost.** Change: [+/- $]. New total: [$]. Deposit paid: [$]. New balance: [$] due [date]

**My decision.** [Yes / Yes with conditions / No / Offer alternatives]

**Client approval.** [Name] approved in writing on [date]

**Done.** Tracker updated [ ] Agreement updated (if needed) [ ] Associate confirmed [ ] Hair checked [ ] New schedule sent as version [ ] on [date]
:::

The AI can fill in the first few lines for you from a messy message. Paste a summary (no names) and ask it to fill in "requested by," "the change" and anything you've told it about timing. Then do the arithmetic and the decision yourself.

## Three response scripts

Most late changes are one of three kinds. Here's a script for each, written as the reply you'd send after you've done the arithmetic. Adapt the details and keep the shape.

### 1. The venue moves your access time

The planner writes to say the room won't be available until 7:30 instead of 7:00. For a morning that already fits exactly, that's 30 minutes short.

> Hi Dana, thanks for letting me know. With access at 7:30 the morning is 30 minutes short for the services booked, so I want to sort it out now rather than on the day. There are two ways to fix it: earlier access to any room near the suite (even a small one would work for the first two bridesmaids), or adding my associate for part of the morning, which would add $40 for her travel. Could you check whether an earlier room is possible? If not, I'll confirm the second option with Amara.

The shape: thank them, state the effect in minutes, offer the options in order of preference with costs, say what you need from them.

### 2. Another person asks for makeup

Someone new wants to join: a mother, a grandmother, a flower girl or a bridesmaid who'd said no. First check timing and your six-service limit. Then:

This example is from a smaller booking, lead L-052: a bride and two bridesmaids, room access at 8:00, ready by 12:00, total $450 with a $120 deposit. The bride's grandmother would like makeup. Four services need 215 minutes including setup and buffer, and the window is 240, so it fits.

> Hi Mei, I'd be happy to do your grandmother's makeup. It's $95 and takes about 40 minutes. I've checked the morning: there's room if she's in the chair at 9:35, just before you, so everyone else's times stay the same. Your new total would be $545 and your balance $425, due a week before the wedding. Could you reply "approved" and I'll send the updated chair times?

If it doesn't fit, say so in the same sentence as the alternative: "On my own I can't fit another 40 minutes before 11:15, but I could bring my associate for part of the morning, which adds $40 for her travel." Never just say no, and never just say yes.

### 3. The photographer moves the time earlier

> Hi Dana, thanks for the heads-up about the 10:45 portraits. I've rerun the timings: with everyone ready by 10:45, the morning is 20 minutes too long for one artist. I could start at 6:25 if the venue allows it, at no extra cost, since that's after my 6:00 earliest start. Or I could keep the current start and ask Theo whether the bride and maid of honor could be photographed first, with the others joining at 11:00. Could you check both with the venue and Theo, and let me know which works best?

Notice that this script offers a no-cost option first. Not every change needs a second artist. Sometimes the right answer is an earlier start that you're happy to do, or a small change of order that the photographer will gladly accept once someone asks.

## When the change comes on the morning

Some changes arrive at 8:40 on the wedding day, in person. A bridesmaid who said no to lashes sits down and says, "Actually, could I have lashes after all?" An aunt appears at the door and asks if you have time "for something quick."

You don't have the luxury of a pause and a spreadsheet, but the three questions still apply, and you can answer them in your head:

- **Timing.** Does it fit in the buffer without putting the ready-by time at risk? Five minutes of lashes probably does. Forty minutes for the aunt almost certainly doesn't, unless she can wait until after the bride is finished.
- **Staffing.** Can you or your associate absorb it without anyone else losing time?
- **Cost.** What's the price, and how will it be paid? Your brief says services added after the balance is paid are paid on the day.

> "Of course. Lashes are $15, and you can pay by card when we're done. I'll pop them on at the end so we stay on time."

Write it down straight away, in your phone, with the price and whether it's been paid. You'll need it for the closeout in Chapter 7, and you won't remember it accurately by the time you've packed your kit.

## What the AI should never say about a change

When you ask a chat tool to help with a change, watch for these, and cut them every time:

- "I've updated your booking." (You haven't until you have the client's approval and have done it.)
- "No problem at all!" (Before you've checked timing.)
- "There won't be any extra charge." (Unless you've decided that.)
- "We'll make it work." (A promise with no plan behind it.)
- "As per our cancellation policy..." followed by a summary. (Policies live in the agreement. Refer to it.)

If these keep appearing, add a line to your brief: "Never agree to a change, say there's no extra cost, or confirm an update. I will tell you what I've agreed."

## Ten-minute action

Think of the last late change you handled, or one you expect for an upcoming booking. Fill in the change request form for it, including the arithmetic. If you said yes too quickly last time, write the reply you'd send now.

Then save the form and the three scripts in your notes app, where you can reach them at 9:12 p.m. on a Thursday.
