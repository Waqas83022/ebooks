# 3. Send the quote and secure the booking {#ch3}

The message arrives while you're cleaning brushes. "Hi! Are you free October 10? It's me and maybe four others. We need to be ready before photos. How much would it be?"

You know your bridal rate. The hard part is working out what the bride is asking you to commit to. Is "before photos" 10 a.m. or noon? Can you get into the venue early enough? Does "maybe four" mean four people want a service, or that four people are still deciding?

In Chapter 2 you answered warmly while you checked, and Amara has now told you everything you asked for. She's getting ready at Hollin House, 18 miles away. The room is available from 7:00 a.m. Everyone must be ready by 11:15. She and four bridesmaids all want makeup, and she'd like to hear about a trial.

It's tempting to open your rate sheet, add it up and send the price. Don't, yet. A quote is a promise about money, but it's also a promise about time, and the time promise is the one that goes wrong.

## Run the timing before you send a price

Most quoting mistakes in bridal makeup aren't arithmetic errors. They're quotes for mornings that don't fit. The bride accepts, pays the deposit and books. Then, months later, you sit down to write the schedule and discover that five services, setup and a buffer don't fit between the time you can get into the room and the time the photographer starts. Now you have three bad choices: start earlier than the venue allows, rush the services, or go back to a booked client and tell her you need a second artist she didn't budget for.

So the timing check comes before the quote. It takes two minutes.

Use the timings in your brief. For this studio that's 15 minutes for setup, 40 minutes per attendant, 60 minutes for the bride and 20 minutes for touch-ups and delays (because there are five services, not six or more).

- Setup: 15 minutes
- Four attendants at 40 minutes: 160 minutes
- Bride: 60 minutes
- Touch-ups and buffer: 20 minutes

That's 255 minutes. The window from 7:00 to 11:15 a.m. is also 255 minutes.

![Amara's morning on paper. The work needs 255 minutes and the window is exactly 255 minutes, so there is no slack.](images/fig-03-timing-window.png){.figure}

It fits, but only just, and only if you can start setting up as soon as the room opens and the first attendant is in the chair by 7:15. Here's how it lays out:

| Time | Appointment |
|---|---|
| 7:00--7:15 | Setup |
| 7:15--7:55 | Attendant 1 |
| 7:55--8:35 | Attendant 2 |
| 8:35--9:15 | Attendant 3 |
| 9:15--9:55 | Attendant 4 |
| 9:55--10:55 | Bride |
| 10:55--11:15 | Touch-ups and buffer |

A schedule with no slack isn't wrong. Plenty of good bridal mornings run on a tight plan. But it tells you what to confirm before you commit.

**Ask whether 7:00 means access to the actual makeup room.** Venues often mean something different by "access." The front door might open at 7:00 while the bridal suite is still being cleaned from the night before. If you lose 15 minutes walking your kit through a building and waiting for a key, you start the first attendant late and the whole morning slips.

**Check the order against the hair schedule.** Every person in the party needs hair and makeup, and nobody can be in two chairs at once. If the hair stylist wants the bride first, your running order has to change. You'll build the joint schedule properly in Chapter 5. For now, you only need to know that a hair stylist exists, and that you'll talk to them before the timetable is final.

**Know what you'll do if it slips.** If access moves to 7:30, you're 30 minutes short with the same services and buffer. You could ask about earlier room access, or check whether your associate artist is free and what a second artist would cost the bride. You don't need to offer either now. You only need to have thought about it, so that if the planner writes in August to say the room isn't available until 7:30, you aren't working it out from scratch.

### Let AI check your timing, then check the AI

You can ask the AI to lay out the morning for you. It's quick, and it's useful as a second pair of eyes. But this is exactly the kind of task where chat tools make small, confident mistakes, so treat its answer as a draft and check it against your own two-minute sum.

::: prompt
**Copy-ready prompt**

Using the timings in my studio brief, check whether this wedding morning fits. Room access 7:00 a.m. Everyone makeup-ready by 11:15 a.m. One artist. Services: bride plus four attendants. Show the total minutes needed, the minutes available, and the difference. Then lay out a proposed schedule with one row per appointment. Flag anything I need to confirm. Don't change any timings from my brief.
:::

If the AI's total is different from yours, find out why before you do anything else. Usually it's left out the setup, used a 30-minute buffer instead of 20, or shortened a service without telling you, to make things fit. That last one matters. A chat tool that's trying to be helpful will sometimes squeeze an appointment to 35 minutes so the schedule works on paper. Your brief says 40. The bride's skin doesn't know you're in a hurry.

## Give AI your approved numbers

Now you can price it. Pull the figures from your own rate sheet, not from memory and not from the AI.

In this example:

- Bridal makeup: $220
- Four attendant services at $95 each: $380
- Travel, Zone 1: $40
- **Wedding-day total: $640**

Check the minimum: $600 of services is well above the $300 minimum, so it doesn't apply.

The deposit rule is 25% of the wedding-day total, rounded up to the nearest $10. A quarter of $640 is $160 exactly, so the deposit is $160, leaving a $480 balance. The optional $110 trial is quoted separately, because it's paid separately and she may not book it.

Do this arithmetic yourself, on paper or in a calculator, before you open the chat tool. The AI can turn these figures into a readable email. Verify the figures yourself and give the tool the checked values. Use the lead ID, and leave out names, the venue address and payment details. Add any client-specific details yourself after you've reviewed the draft.

::: prompt
**Copy-ready prompt**

Draft a friendly bridal makeup quote email in 150 words or fewer using only the details below. Use [Client first name] as the greeting. Make the four attendant services and the travel charge easy to see. Show the optional trial separately. Describe the schedule as proposed. State that the booking is confirmed once the agreement is accepted and the deposit is verified. Leave any missing information in brackets. Flag contradictions separately. Do not invent fees, deadlines, links or policies.

Details: October 10. Bride $220. Four attendants at $95 each, subtotal $380. Travel $40. Wedding-day total $640. Deposit $160 applied to that total. Wedding-day balance $480, due 7 days before the wedding. Optional trial $110, billed separately if booked. Proposed setup at 7:00 a.m., all makeup ready by 11:15 a.m., subject to confirming room access and coordination with hair. Quote valid for 14 days. Use [secure booking link] as a placeholder.
:::

Every line of that prompt is there for a reason. "Using only the details below" stops the tool adding a fee it thinks you probably charge. "Describe the schedule as proposed" stops it promising a time. "Flag contradictions separately" means that if you made a typo, say $95 times four written as $360, the tool tells you instead of silently picking one of the two numbers. And "Do not invent... links or policies" stops the most common problem of all, a made-up cancellation clause that sounds official and doesn't match your agreement.

Before sending, check the date, the rates, the schedule wording, the payment link and any terms in the agreement. In your tracker, enter the quote version (v1), the date sent, the total and a follow-up date.

### What to look for in the draft

When you read a quote draft, look out for these. They're the slips that come up again and again.

**A word that changes the deal.** "Your date is reserved." "I've held the 10th for you." "Non-refundable deposit." Unless that exact wording is in your agreement and your brief, cut it. The words "reserve" and "hold" are the most dangerous, because a bride who reads them will reasonably believe she doesn't need to rush.

**A deadline you didn't give it.** If you didn't include a validity period, some tools will invent one, usually 30 days. If you did include one, make sure it's yours.

**Arithmetic that's slightly off.** Check every line and every total, even though you gave it the numbers. Occasionally a tool will recalculate a figure you supplied and get it wrong, or round a deposit differently from your rule.

**A softened schedule.** "We'll have you ready in plenty of time!" isn't a schedule. Neither is "I'll arrive at 7." Your quote should say the plan is proposed and name what still needs confirming.

**Extra friendliness that costs money.** "I'd be happy to throw in lashes for your bridesmaids!" Some tools add small gifts to make a message feel generous. Lashes for four attendants is $60 of your time and product.

## Make the follow-up easy to answer

A few days pass with no reply. That's normal. Brides collect quotes, talk to their partner, wait for a bridesmaid to confirm and check with a parent who's helping to pay.

The natural instinct is to write "Just checking in!" Resist it. A vague check-in leaves the bride to work out what you need from her, so it's easy to put off. Ask about a decision she can make.

In this example, the open questions are whether she wants the four bridesmaid services as quoted and whether she'd like the optional trial. Check your calendar before mentioning availability, in case the date has been booked by someone else in the meantime.

::: prompt
**Copy-ready prompt**

Lead L-031. I sent a quote 4 days ago for October 10, bride plus four attendants, total $640, optional trial $110. No reply yet. October 10 is still open in my calendar today.

Draft a short follow-up (under 70 words) that asks one easy question: whether she has any questions, or whether she'd like trial dates. Mention I can update the quote if the number of attendants has changed. No pressure, no "just checking in," no claims that the date is in demand.
:::

> Hi Amara, I wanted to see whether you had any questions about the makeup quote for October 10. If you're deciding whether to include a trial, I'm happy to send a few dates. If the number of bridesmaids has changed, let me know and I can update the quote and timings.

That last line does quiet work. If a fifth bridesmaid has appeared, you want to hear about it now, not on the morning.

If the bride does ask to add another attendant, check timing before you update the price. Another 40-minute service takes this schedule 40 minutes past the ready-by time, unless you can change the access time, the staffing or the service plan with the client's agreement. That's a decision for you. It might mean an earlier start (if the venue allows it), a second artist for part of the morning, or a conversation about what the photographer actually needs at 11:15. Don't simply add $95 to the total and send it back.

### How many follow-ups?

Two is usually right. The first goes three or four days after the quote, asking a specific question. The second goes a couple of days before the quote expires, and says so plainly: "Your quote is valid until Friday. After that I'll need to check availability again before confirming the date." That's not pressure. It's the truth, and it helps a bride who's been meaning to reply.

After that, mark the lead "Closed" in your tracker with a note. Many artists find this hard, because it feels like giving up. It isn't. A closed lead that comes back in a month is still a happy surprise. An open lead that sits in your tracker for six months is clutter that hides the inquiries that need you.

### When she asks you to hold the date

Sooner or later a bride will write: "We definitely want to book, can you hold the date until payday?" It's a reasonable request, and your answer is a decision. Some artists hold for 48 hours. Some never hold. Whatever you decide, put it in your brief so the AI doesn't improvise.

If your policy is no hold, the reply can still be warm:

> Hi Amara, I'm so glad you'd like to book. I can't hold dates without the agreement and deposit, only because it wouldn't be fair to other couples asking about October 10. If anything changes before payday, I'll let you know straight away, and I'd love to confirm you as soon as you're ready.

Promise only what you'll do. "I'll let you know if someone else asks" is a promise. Make it only if you'll keep it.

## Confirm from your own records

Amara replies a week later. "Booked! I've signed the contract and paid the deposit. So excited!"

This is the moment where a chat tool can do the most damage, because the natural, warm, helpful reply is "Wonderful, you're all booked!" And you may not know that yet.

When a client says "I've paid," open your payment system and check the amount and the status. Don't rely on a screenshot, a forwarded email or the client's word, however sincere. Payments can be pending, reversed, sent to an old account or made for the wrong amount. Check the agreement separately, in whatever system you use for contracts.

In this studio, a date becomes booked only once both checks are complete:

- **Agreement accepted and deposit verified:** Booked.
- **Agreement accepted, payment pending or not yet visible:** Awaiting deposit.
- **Payment verified, agreement not yet accepted:** record the payment and mark Awaiting agreement.

![A date is booked only when both checks pass. Each is recorded separately in the tracker.](images/fig-03-status-flow.png){.figure}

Keep the payment details in your booking system. The AI never needs to see them, and the confirmation message only needs the amount.

In Amara's case, both checks pass. The agreement shows as accepted, and the $160 deposit has cleared. Now, and only now, you can confirm.

::: prompt
**Copy-ready prompt**

Lead L-031 is now booked. I've verified the agreement and the $160 deposit myself. Draft a short confirmation (under 100 words). October 10, bride plus four attendants. Remaining balance $480, due 7 days before the wedding. Working toward an 11:15 a.m. ready-by time. I'll share the final running order once room access and hair timing are confirmed. Offer to send trial dates.
:::

> Hi Amara, your October 10 bridal makeup booking is confirmed for you and four bridesmaids. I've received your agreement and your $160 deposit. Your remaining balance is $480, due a week before the wedding. We're working toward an 11:15 a.m. ready-by time, and I'll share the final running order once room access and hair timing are confirmed. If you'd like to book the optional trial, I can send you some dates.

Move the date to "Booked" in your tracker and block it on your calendar. Set your next action while the thread is open. Here, it's two things: send trial dates, and confirm room access and the running order with the hair stylist. Give each a date.

## The deposit checklist

Run this every time a client says she's booked, before you send any confirmation.

::: checklist
**Before you confirm a booking**

1. **Agreement.** Accepted in my contract system? Correct date, services and total?
2. **Payment.** Showing as received (not pending) in my payment system? Correct amount?
3. **Tracker.** Agreement date and deposit date recorded in separate fields? Status updated?
4. **Calendar.** Date blocked, with the lead ID and ready-by time?
5. **Balance.** Remaining balance and due date written in the tracker?
6. **Next action.** Named and dated (trial, access, hair, or all three)?
:::

Track the agreement and the deposit as separate fields even if you keep one overall status. It takes a few extra seconds, and the first time a client insists she signed in May when your system says she didn't, you'll be glad you did.

## Try it on your next inquiry

Choose a past inquiry and replace any identifying details with placeholders. Check the date, headcount and ready-by time. Calculate the timing first, then the quote, from your own rates. Draft the email using the quote prompt in this chapter, then compare the draft against your numbers and your terms line by line.

Record one next action. If the booking is waiting on something, you should be able to name exactly what it is.
