# A last word: the artist in the loop {.unnumbered #last-word}

At no point in this book has the AI made a decision.

It wrote Amara's first reply, but you checked the calendar. It drafted her quote, but you did the arithmetic and noticed the morning had no slack. It turned your trial notes into a recap, but you knew which shimmer she meant. It flagged that her mother's makeup and the earlier portraits added up to a morning 70 minutes too long, but you were the one who messaged Nadia, chose what to offer, and asked Amara to approve it in writing. On the morning itself, the AI wasn't there at all. You were.

That's the right way round. The tools will keep getting better. They'll get faster, they'll get better at arithmetic and they'll connect to more of your calendar and your booking system. But a bride doesn't book you because your emails arrive quickly. She books you because she trusts you with her face on one of the most photographed days of her life, and with her mother, her sister and her best friends. Trust comes from the commitments you keep: the price you quoted, the time you promised, the look you agreed.

So let the AI take the typing. Keep the promises for yourself.

And the next time a message arrives at 11:40 p.m. asking, "Are you free October 10? It's me and maybe four others," you'll know exactly what to do. Check the calendar. Ask for what changes the price and the time. Then go to sleep.

Muhammad Waqas is the author of *The Archer and the Wind: How to Keep Going When Hard Work Isn't Enough*. *AI for Bridal Makeup Artists* is built on a simple idea: the artists with the calmest wedding mornings aren't the fastest typists.
