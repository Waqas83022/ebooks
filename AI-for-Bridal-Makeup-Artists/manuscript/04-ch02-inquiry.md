# 2. Turn a vague inquiry into a useful reply {#ch2}

Amara's message arrives in your Instagram DMs on a Sunday afternoon:

"Hi! Are you free October 10? It's me and maybe four others. We need to be ready before photos. How much would it be?"

It's a friendly message, and a typical one. It also gives you exactly one fact you can rely on, the date. "Maybe four others" could mean four people who definitely want makeup, or four bridesmaids of whom two are thinking about it. "Before photos" could mean 10:00 a.m. or 1:30 p.m. And "How much?" depends on all of it, plus where she's getting ready, which she hasn't said.

You have two bad options and one good one. You can send a price straight away, which feels responsive but is likely to be wrong. You can send a long questionnaire, which feels thorough but asks a stranger to do homework before she knows if she likes you. Or you can send a short, warm reply that answers what you can and asks only for what changes the price or the timing.

This chapter is about that third option, for the three kinds of inquiry you'll get most often: the date is open, the date is already booked, and the inquiry is too uncertain to quote yet. It finishes with a tracker that keeps every inquiry in one place, so a bride who messaged on Instagram doesn't get lost while you're answering a planner's email.

## Ask only for what affects availability and price

When you're tempted to ask a new inquiry lots of questions, sort each one into one of two piles. Does the answer change whether you can do the wedding, or what it costs? If yes, ask now. If no, it can wait until she's booked, or at least until she has your quote.

For most bridal artists, five things belong in the first pile.

**Date.** Without it, you can't check your calendar. Everything else waits on this.

**Location.** Where she's getting ready tells you the travel charge and starts the timing question, because you also need to know when vendors can get into the room.

**Deadline.** The time everyone must be makeup-ready. Ask whether there's an earlier commitment, such as a first look or a departure for the ceremony, because the earliest deadline is the one that counts.

**Headcount.** How many people want makeup, including the bride, and whether any are undecided. Undecided people affect timing more than price, but they affect both.

**Trial.** Whether she'd like information about a trial. You ask now because it's a separate service with its own booking and it's often what tips a bride from "thinking about it" to "booked."

Everything else goes in the second pile: skin type, inspiration photos, allergies, budget, how she heard about you, her hair stylist's name, the color of the bridesmaid dresses. Some of those matter a great deal for the work itself. You'll gather them at the trial and in the wedding-day brief (Chapter 4). Asking for them in the first reply just makes the message longer and the answer slower.

There's one exception. If a client volunteers useful information, use it. A bride who opens with "We're getting ready at the Grand Hotel and need to be done by 11" has answered two of your five questions. Acknowledge that she's told you and only ask for the rest. Nothing irritates a client more than being asked for something she's already said.

## The three replies

The right reply depends on what your calendar says, so the calendar comes first, every time. Then pick one of three paths.

![Three reply paths for a new inquiry. Check the calendar before choosing.](images/fig-02-reply-paths.png){.figure}

### When the date is open

This is the reply Amara gets. You've checked your calendar and October 10 is free. You don't yet know if you can make the timing work, so you say the date is open, which is true, rather than that you're available, which is a promise.

Before writing anything, retell the inquiry to the AI in your own words. Don't paste her message.

::: prompt
**Copy-ready prompt**

Use my studio brief. Lead L-031, Instagram DM. Bride asks if I'm free October 10 for herself "and maybe four others," everyone ready "before photos," and asks the price. I've checked my calendar: October 10 is currently open.

Draft a short DM reply, under 70 words. Thank her. Say the date is currently open in my calendar, without promising to hold it. Ask for the getting-ready venue, the time everyone needs to be ready for photos, and how many people would like makeup including her. Say I'll check the timing and send an itemized quote once I have those details. Use [Client first name].
:::

The draft comes back. You read it against the pre-send checklist, cut an exclamation mark, and send:

> Hi Amara! Thanks for getting in touch. October 10 is currently open in my calendar. Could you tell me where you'll be getting ready, what time everyone needs to be ready for photos, and how many people would like makeup, including you? Once I have those details I'll check the timing and send you an itemized quote.

It's 57 words. It answers her first question, tells her what happens next, and asks three things she can answer from her phone without looking anything up. It doesn't mention the trial yet, because she'll see it on the quote and the DM is already doing enough.

Notice what you didn't say. You didn't say "I'm available." You didn't say "I'll pencil you in." You didn't give a price. Each of those would have been a small commitment made before you had the facts.

### When the date is already booked

A booked date is still a chance to leave a good impression, because brides talk to each other and the bridesmaid in this party might be next year's bride.

Keep it short and kind. If you work with an associate or a group of artists you trust, the brief should say whether you can suggest them. Recommending someone is a decision, so it belongs on your "Hand back to me" list unless you've already decided that you always do it.

::: prompt
**Copy-ready prompt**

Use my studio brief. Lead L-032, contact form. Bride asks about June 6 for herself and two bridesmaids. I'm already booked that day. I'm happy to pass on details of [Associate artist] who may be free; she should contact her directly.

Draft a short email reply, under 90 words. Thank her, say I'm already booked on June 6, and offer the associate's details. Wish her well. Don't apologize more than once.
:::

> Hi Priya,
>
> Thank you so much for thinking of me for your wedding. I'm already booked on June 6, so I can't take this one, I'm afraid. My colleague Nadia does beautiful, long-wearing bridal makeup and may be free. You can reach her at [contact]. Mention that I sent you.
>
> Wishing you a wonderful day,\
> [Artist first name]

Don't skip the tracker for a booked date. A note that says "L-032, June 6, declined, booked" tells you, in March next year, how many inquiries you turned away for June Saturdays. That's how you know when it's time to raise your rates or bring in a second artist.

### When the inquiry is too uncertain to quote

Some inquiries arrive before the bride knows the basics. "Hi, how much do you charge for bridal? Getting married sometime next summer." There's no date to check and no headcount to price.

You could ignore these, but they often become real bookings a few months later, and the artist who answered helpfully the first time has an advantage. Give her what she can use now, which is a guide to your rates, and tell her exactly what you need to give her a real quote.

Your rates are a rule in your brief, so the AI can quote them. Make sure the reply describes them as your current rates and doesn't add them up into a total for a party you haven't been told about.

::: prompt
**Copy-ready prompt**

Use my studio brief. Lead L-033, Instagram DM. Asks "how much for bridal," wedding "sometime next summer," no other details.

Draft a short DM reply, under 80 words. Give my current bridal and attendant rates and say travel depends on the getting-ready location. Say I can check availability and send an itemized quote once she has a date. Don't calculate a total. Don't mention discounts or holds.
:::

> Hi! Congratulations. My current rates are $220 for bridal makeup and $95 for each bridesmaid or family member, plus travel, which depends on where you're getting ready. Once you have a date, send it over with your venue and how many people would like makeup, and I'll check my calendar and send you an itemized quote.

In your tracker, give her a status of "Needs details" and a follow-up date a few weeks out. If she replies with a date, she's a new conversation with a real inquiry.

## Different channels, same five questions

Inquiries reach you in different ways. The questions stay the same; the length and shape change.

**Instagram and other DMs** need short replies. People read them on a phone, often in between other things. Aim for under 70 words and never more than three questions. If you need a fourth fact, ask for it in the next message.

**Email** gives you a little more room, and a bride inquiring by email often expects a slightly fuller reply. You can include a line about how bookings work, but keep the questions to the five that matter.

**Website contact forms** let you collect the five answers before she even writes to you. If your form currently has a single "Message" box, replace it with the intake form at the end of this chapter. You'll get better inquiries, and the ones that arrive complete can go straight to a quote.

**Referrals** often come through a past client, a planner or a venue, sometimes in a group chat or a forwarded message. Thank the person who referred her by name if you know it. It costs you four words and it matters a lot to the person who recommended you.

**Voice notes** are the one format you shouldn't hand to the AI as they are. Listen, then retell the facts in a sentence or two. That has the useful side effect of making you notice what she didn't say.

## Price shoppers and "Can you match this?"

Some inquiries are just a request for a number. "Hi, what are your prices?" with nothing else. Answer the same way as an uncertain inquiry: give your current rates and ask for the facts you need for a full quote. Some of these people will disappear. Some will come back with a date. You can't tell which in advance, so give both the same courtesy.

Price-matching requests are different, because matching a price is a discount, and discounts are decisions. If a bride sends you a screenshot of another artist's price list and asks whether you can match it, decide first. Then let the AI help you say it well.

If your answer is no, the reply should explain what's included in your price and leave the door open without sounding defensive:

::: prompt
**Copy-ready prompt**

Use my studio brief. Lead L-034. Bride says another artist quoted $180 for bridal and asks if I can match it. I don't price-match. Draft a warm, short reply (under 90 words) that says my bridal rate is $220 and explains what's included, using only what's in my brief. Don't criticise the other artist. Don't offer a discount. End by asking for her date and venue so I can send a full quote.
:::

Read the result carefully. AI tools have a habit of slipping in a soft concession ("I'd be happy to discuss options...") that sounds harmless and invites a negotiation you've already decided against. Cut it.

## One tracker for every inquiry

By the time you've answered a week of inquiries, they're spread across Instagram, email, your website form, WhatsApp and the notes app where you wrote down the planner's phone call. The cure is one tracker that every inquiry goes into, regardless of where it came from.

It can be a spreadsheet or the leads section of a booking system. What matters is that there's one row per inquiry and that each row tells you where the booking stands and what happens next.

![A simple inquiry tracker. One row per lead, one status and one next action each.](images/fig-02-tracker.png){.figure}

Here are the columns that earn their place:

- **Lead ID and source.** L-031, Instagram.
- **Received.** The date the inquiry arrived.
- **Wedding date.**
- **Services.** "Bride + 4" is enough.
- **Access and ready-by.** Blank until you know them.
- **Quote.** Version, date sent and total.
- **Agreement.** Date accepted.
- **Deposit.** Amount required and date verified.
- **Status.** One of a fixed list (below).
- **Next action and due date.** The single next thing that needs to happen, and when.

Keep the client's real name and contact details in your tracker or booking system, not in the AI tool. The tracker is your private record. The AI only ever sees the lead ID.

Use a fixed set of statuses, so the tracker can be sorted and counted later:

*New. Needs details. Quote sent. Awaiting agreement. Awaiting deposit. Booked. Declined. Closed.*

"Declined" is for dates you turned down, so you can count them. "Closed" is for leads that went quiet, chose someone else, or finished after the wedding. Chapter 7 uses these statuses to work out your conversion rates, so it's worth being consistent from the start.

The most useful column is the last one. A next action that says "Wait" is not an action. "Send quote, Monday" is. "Follow up if no reply, Thursday" is. If you can't name the next action, the inquiry is either finished (close it) or you're missing something (find out what).

### Letting AI turn a messy thread into a tracker row

When a conversation has gone back and forth a few times, the facts end up scattered. A bride gives her venue in one message, changes her headcount in another and mentions the ready-by time in a voice note. You can use the AI to pull it together, as long as you strip identifying details first.

::: prompt
**Copy-ready prompt**

Here's a summary of an inquiry thread for lead L-031, with names removed. Turn it into one tracker row with these fields: lead ID, source, wedding date, services, access time, ready-by time, quote status, next action. Leave any field I haven't given you blank. Don't guess. Under the row, list anything that's contradictory or still unknown.

[Retell the thread in a few lines]
:::

The list underneath is the part that pays. "Headcount was 'maybe four' in the first message and 'four' in the second. Access time unknown." That's your next message, written for you.

## Amara, two days later

Amara replies on Tuesday:

"Thank you! We're getting ready at Hollin House. The room's available from 7 and we all need to be ready by 11:15 because the photographer is starting then. It's me and four bridesmaids, all definitely want makeup. Would love to hear about a trial too!"

That's all five questions answered. Hollin House is 18 miles from the studio, so travel is Zone 1. Her tracker row now reads:

*L-031 · Instagram · Received Sun · Oct 10 · Bride + 4 · Access 7:00 · Ready 11:15 · Trial: interested · Status: New → Quote in progress · Next: check timing, send quote, Wed.*

You have what you need to quote. But before any price goes out, there's one more thing to check, and it's the reason so many bridal mornings run late. That's Chapter 3.

## The intake form

If your website or booking system lets you build a form, use these five questions. Each one maps to a column in your tracker, so a complete form can be copied straight in.

::: template
**Wedding makeup inquiry**

1. What is your wedding date?
2. Where will you be getting ready, and what time can suppliers get into the room?
3. What time does everyone need to be makeup-ready? Is there an earlier photo, first look or departure time?
4. How many people would like makeup, including you? Are any still undecided?
5. Would you like information about a bridal trial?

Anything else you'd like me to know? (optional)
:::

Keep the last box optional. Some brides will tell you about their skin, their dress, their Pinterest board and their mother-in-law in that box, and that's fine. Most will leave it blank and you'll gather the rest at the trial.

## Ten-minute action

Go back through your messages from the last two weeks and find every inquiry that hasn't reached a quote or a clear "no." Give each a lead ID and a row in a tracker, even if it's a quick spreadsheet with five columns. For each one, write a single next action and a date.

Then pick the oldest one and answer it using the right path from this chapter. You'll probably find that at least one inquiry you'd mentally written off only needed a single clear question.
