# The focus toolkit {.unnumbered}

These are the planning pages and worksheets used throughout the book. If you're reading on a Kindle or phone, copy the questions into a notebook or a notes app. If you have the paperback, you can write straight onto the page or photocopy it for your own use.

Keep them simple. A plan you use every day is worth more than a beautiful one you abandon.

## 2. The focus session log

Fill in one line after each session. Review it weekly. (Chapters 5 and 12)

::: worksheet
For each session, note:

- Date and start time
- Length (minutes)
- Task and target
- Target reached? (yes, partly, no)
- Number of times my mind wandered (roughly)
- Main distraction
- Energy level, 1 to 5

**Weekly totals:** number of completed sessions, total focused minutes, best time of day, most common distraction.
:::

## 5. The digital reset checklist

Work through this once, then check it again at each monthly reset. (Chapter 6)

::: worksheet
- Notifications turned off for every app that fails the test "Does a human being need me, right now?"
- Work or study focus mode set up and scheduled
- Sleep mode set up and scheduled
- Time-swallowing apps moved off the home screen
- Worst app deleted from the phone, or logged out after each use
- Grayscale tried for at least one week
- A home for the phone during focus sessions, away from the desk
- Phone charging outside the bedroom, with an alarm clock instead
- Checking windows chosen for social media and non-urgent messages
- Replacements chosen for boredom, waiting, breaks and winding down
:::

## 6. The if-then planner

Write a plan for each of your most common obstacles. Say each one to yourself once or twice. (Chapter 11)

::: worksheet
**To start important work:**
If it is [time, place or event], then I will [small, specific first action].

**To handle distraction:**
If I feel the urge to [distraction], then I will [response].

**To handle interruption:**
If [person] asks me to [request] during my focus time, then I will [response].

**To handle low energy:**
If I'm too tired for my full session, then I will [minimum version].

**To recover from a slip:**
If I miss my habit one day, then the next day I will [minimum version].
:::

## 7. The WOOP sheet

Use this for a big goal, a week or a single day. It takes about five minutes. (Chapters 4 and 14)

::: worksheet
**Wish:** What do I want to achieve? (Challenging but possible.)

**Outcome:** What's the best result of achieving it? How will it feel? Picture it for a minute.

**Obstacle:** What's the main thing inside me that could stop me? Picture it clearly.

**Plan:** If [obstacle], then I will [action].
:::

## 12. The shutdown ritual

Ten to fifteen minutes at the end of every workday. (Chapter 17)

::: worksheet
1. Check inbox and messages one last time; capture anything that needs doing.
2. Look at tomorrow's calendar and task list.
3. Choose tomorrow's MIT and write it down.
4. Leave a note on any unfinished task: where I stopped and what comes next.
5. Tidy the desk.
6. Say my shutdown phrase:
7. Close the laptop, switch off work notifications, leave the workspace.
:::

## 13. Which technique should I try?

When something isn't working, find your problem below and try the suggested technique first.

::: worksheet
**I keep reaching for my phone.** Turn off notifications, give the phone a home outside the room, move apps off the home screen, try grayscale. (Chapter 6)

**I can't decide where to start.** Sort your list with the urgent-important matrix and choose one MIT. (Chapter 9)

**The task feels too big.** Find the next physical action, or break the task into session-sized steps. (Chapter 11)

**I dread starting.** Name the feeling, then do a two-minute start or a ten-minute agreement. (Chapters 2 and 11)

**I'm afraid it won't be good enough.** Write an ugly first draft against a timer, and set a "good enough" standard in advance. (Chapters 4 and 11)

**My mind keeps wandering.** Use a parking page, notice and name each drift, and try five minutes of breath-focused practice daily. (Chapters 4 and 5)

**I keep worrying about other things.** Schedule a daily worry time and write worries down when they appear. (Chapter 4)

**I can only focus for a few minutes.** Start low on the focus ladder and build up slowly. (Chapter 5)

**I get interrupted constantly.** Batch shallow work, block focus time in your calendar, agree on what's urgent, use a visible signal. (Chapters 8 and 19)

**My plans always collapse.** Plan less, add 50 percent to estimates, and add buffer blocks. (Chapter 10)

**I'm busy all day but nothing important moves.** Do your MIT first, before email, and protect one deep work block a day. (Chapters 8 and 9)

**I start well and then stop.** Shrink the habit, stack it on an existing one, track it, and never miss twice. (Chapters 15 and 16)

**One bad day ruins my week.** Use the reset routine and the friend test. (Chapter 15)

**I'm exhausted.** Check sleep, caffeine, breaks and boundaries before anything else. Watch for signs of burnout. (Chapter 17)
:::
