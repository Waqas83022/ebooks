# Afterword: the first session

It is easy to finish a book like this and feel that you have done something. You have learned about offers, pricing, quality gates and tax set-asides. You may have underlined passages and made notes. And on Monday morning you will go back to work, and in the evening you will be tired, and the business will remain an idea.

So let me make one request. Before you put this book down, open your calendar and put in your first two-hour session. Give it a date, a time and a place. Write the first task next to it. If you have done nothing else so far, make it the skill inventory from Chapter 4. If you have been following along, make it the next unfinished item on the pilot readiness checklist.

That session is the whole business at this point. Everything else follows from it.

A few things to remember as you go.

The work that earns money is the work AI can't do for you: asking the right questions, noticing what is missing, checking what is true, fitting the result to a real person's situation and standing behind it. The tools will keep changing. That part won't.

Your job is your foundation. Protect it with a clean line, an honest conversation and a capacity cap, and it will fund your experiments for as long as you need.

Small and specific beats big and vague. One offer, one buyer, one clear outcome, one price.

Count every hour. The numbers will tell you things your hopes won't.

And give it a fair test. Ten weeks, five hours a week, about fifty hours in total. At the end you will know far more than you know now, whatever the decision.

I hope your first client is as pleased as Gary was in his driveway, holding a procedure he'd been meaning to write for six years. And I hope that, a year from now, you look at your calendar and see a small, steady business that is entirely yours.
